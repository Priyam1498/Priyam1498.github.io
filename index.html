About

Quantum algorithms for sensing and network control

I am a third-year PhD student in the Department of Informatics and Networked Systems at the University of Pittsburgh, advised by Dr. Kaushik P. Seshadreesan.

My research focuses on variational quantum algorithms for quantum sensing and multiparameter estimation, and reinforcement-learning-based control of quantum networks. I am interested in how physical interactions, restricted control resources, and realistic noise and memory constraints influence the performance of quantum-information protocols.

Selected research

My work combines quantum-information theory, physical modeling, numerical simulation, and classical optimization. The projects below address questions in quantum metrology and entanglement distribution.

01 / QUANTUM SENSING

Variational probe and measurement optimization

Designing quantum probe states and readout circuits for structured phase estimation with dipolar-interacting spin systems, under finite-depth and restricted-control constraints.

Comparing sequential and joint optimization through classical and quantum Fisher information, including the contribution of measurement design to attainable sensitivity.

Earlier preprint Research details

02 / QUANTUM METROLOGY

Joint magnetometry and gradiometry

Investigating simultaneous estimation of magnetic-field offsets and spatial gradients using variational quantum probes on dipolar spin chains.

A five-spin, three-layer configuration achieved a 4.2-fold improvement over the standard-quantum-limit determinant benchmark in the studied model.

Preprint Research details

03 / QUANTUM NETWORKING

Reinforcement learning for quantum-network control

Developing reinforcement-learning policies for link-level quantum-memory management and studying sequential versus synchronized entanglement swapping under finite memory coherence.

Characterizing how memory coherence and link heterogeneity affect entanglement distribution and secret-key-rate performance.

Research interests

My broader interests include quantum algorithms, quantum sensing and metrology, multiparameter quantum estimation, quantum simulation, distributed quantum sensing, quantum networking, and learning-based methods for quantum systems.

I approach these problems primarily through analytical modeling and computational research. My work involves Python-based scientific simulations, variational optimization, reinforcement learning, and high-performance computing.

Before Pittsburgh

I completed an integrated BS-MS degree in physics at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Bhopal.

My master's thesis, supervised by Dr. Rohan Singh, investigated two-dimensional coherent spectroscopy with chirped and unchirped pulses. I numerically solved optical Bloch equations and gained bench-side experience with ultrafast spectroscopy.

I subsequently worked with Dr. Ankur Raina on a measurement-based quantum computing approach to surface-code encoding. Earlier, during a summer research internship with Dr. Srijit Bhattacharjee at IIIT Allahabad, I studied entanglement entropy in coupled harmonic-oscillator systems.
