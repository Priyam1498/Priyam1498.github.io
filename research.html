Research

Two threads, one question

Variational optimization for design problems, reinforcement learning for control problems. Both ask what a noisy, finite, partly controllable quantum device can actually deliver.

variational quantum sensing

Probes and measurements you optimize instead of derive

Textbook metrology hands you GHZ states and parity measurements. Real dipolar spin platforms hand you a fixed interaction and limited control. I set up the probe state, the encoding, and the readout as a parameterized circuit, then optimize the Fisher information directly with CMA-ES over PennyLane and JAX simulations. Learned probes approach entanglement-enhanced precision bounds and reach the Heisenberg limit for uniform phase encoding.

The multiparameter case is harder than it looks. The quantum Fisher information of a GHZ state is rank deficient, so joint magnetometry and gradiometry needs a different objective, and optimizing det(F) gets there.

reinforcement learning for network control

Deciding when to swap and when to wait

Entanglement distribution is a scheduling problem under decoherence: hold a link too long and it decays, swap too early and you waste it. I built a two-layer simulator in PyTorch and Gymnasium. An identical utility-maximizing agent runs on every link, delivering Werner pairs into per-link buffers, and a network-layer controller then chooses between swapping hop by hop, which leaves partial chains ageing in storage, and holding until every buffer is valid and swapping them together. Sweeping coherence time as SLURM array jobs on the Pitt CRC cluster exposes a regime structure in the ratio of external coherence time to link generation time, which governs the penalty for connection-less operation.
