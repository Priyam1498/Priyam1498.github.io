Research

Quantum sensing, estimation, and network control

My research investigates how quantum-information protocols can be designed and optimized under constraints imposed by physical interactions, available control operations, measurement design, and finite quantum-memory coherence.

I work primarily on two interconnected areas: quantum sensing and metrology, where I study variational probe preparation and measurement strategies, and quantum networking, where I investigate learning-based link control and entanglement distribution.

My approach combines analytical modeling, quantum-information methods, numerical simulation, and optimization. I am particularly interested in identifying which physical or operational constraints determine achievable performance, and how optimization can help reveal those limits.

Variational quantum sensing

Structured phase estimation: variational probe and readout design

Quantum-enhanced sensing depends not only on how much information a probe state can encode, but also on whether the available measurement operations can extract that information. I investigate this distinction in dipolar-interacting spin systems with restricted, finite-depth control.

Research question

How does including the measurement circuit during variational probe optimization affect the achievable precision of structured single-parameter quantum sensing?

Approach

I model systems of 2–8 dipolar-interacting spin-1/2 particles using PennyLane and JAX. Parameterized circuits prepare the sensing probe through alternating interaction dynamics and collective control rotations. CMA-ES is used for numerical optimization.

For structured phase estimation, the parameter is encoded through prescribed spatial profiles across the spins. The sensing performance is evaluated using classical Fisher information (CFI), which depends on the measurement probabilities, and quantum Fisher information (QFI), which characterizes the local information available in the quantum state.

I compare two optimization strategies using matched encoder and readout circuit resources.

Main findings

  • Joint probe–readout optimization improved CFI by 14.9% relative to sequential optimization for uniform encoding with eight spins and three circuit layers.
  • Fixed-probe readout re-optimization and direct-QFI optimization help distinguish limitations of probe preparation from limitations of measurement extraction.
  • Comparisons with Ramsey-based measurements and simpler measurement choices clarify when optimized probes require additional readout control.

Scientific perspective

A probe with high QFI does not necessarily yield equally high CFI under a restricted measurement. Probe optimization and measurement design must therefore be assessed together when evaluating implementable sensing protocols.

These results come from numerical simulations of the specified spin models. The optimized circuits have not been experimentally implemented in this work. A journal manuscript extending the QCE 2025 study is in preparation.

Multiparameter quantum metrology

Joint magnetometry and gradiometry on dipolar spin chains

Many sensing applications require estimating multiple physical quantities from the same quantum system. This introduces questions beyond maximizing sensitivity to a single parameter, including identifiability and trade-offs between different estimation directions.

Research question

Can variationally prepared quantum probes improve the simultaneous estimation of a magnetic-field offset and spatial gradient, and how should their joint sensitivity be quantified?

Approach

I study joint magnetometry and gradiometry using dipolar-interacting spin chains with variational probe-preparation circuits. The sensing parameters are a uniform magnetic-field component and a spatial field gradient.

Performance is characterized through the Fisher-information matrix, rather than a scalar sensitivity for either parameter alone. The determinant provides a measure of joint local information volume when the information matrix is nonsingular.

A central theoretical observation is that a conventional GHZ probe produces a rank-one QFI matrix for the two commuting sensing generators in this model. It is therefore insufficient for independently identifying both parameters locally.

Main result

For a five-spin system with three variational circuit layers, the optimized joint-estimation performance reached approximately 92% of the studied diagonal-encoder QFI determinant benchmark, corresponding to about 4.2 times the standard-quantum-limit determinant.

Significance

This problem demonstrates why sensitivity to an individual parameter is not sufficient for evaluating multiparameter sensing. Highly entangled states can be useful for one estimation direction while failing to distinguish multiple parameter combinations.

Variational optimization provides a numerical approach to exploring probe structures that distribute sensitivity across the parameters of interest, subject to the specified interaction and circuit constraints.

The result is specific to the studied finite spin system and optimization setting. The quoted benchmark does not represent a demonstrated universal optimum across all possible probe preparations.

Reinforcement learning and quantum networking

Quantum-memory control and entanglement-swapping strategies

Long-distance quantum communication relies on generating, storing, purifying, and swapping entanglement across intermediate nodes. The performance of these operations depends on stochastic link generation and finite memory coherence.

Research question

How do link-level quantum-memory control and network-level entanglement-swapping strategies interact, and when does one swapping protocol outperform another under finite-coherence constraints?

Approach

I developed a simulation framework using Python, PyTorch, and Gymnasium to investigate quantum repeater chains. At the elementary-link level, classical REINFORCE agents learn policies for managing entangled-pair resources.

The link controller selects among waiting, discarding, purification, and delivery decisions. The resulting entangled pairs are supplied to a network-level simulator, where different prescribed swapping strategies can be evaluated.

In the ACM PQQS 2026 study, the same link-control policy is used for both swapping strategies at each operating point. Thus, the comparison concerns the network protocol rather than an RL agent learning to choose between protocols.

Main findings

  • In the studied four-hop chain, synchronized swapping remains viable in short-memory regimes where sequential swapping is strongly suppressed.
  • Sequential swapping can provide an advantage at sufficiently long memory coherence times, reaching 19.1% higher secret-key rate in a tested homogeneous 5 km-per-link configuration.
  • A fidelity-based analytical condition characterizes the onset of viable sequential swapping. The ratio of memory coherence time to link latency helps organize the observed regimes.
  • Heterogeneous link lengths introduce additional performance effects associated with bottleneck placement and memory occupancy.
  • The link-level RL study also identified an over-purification failure mode that could prevent successful training.

Scientific perspective

A swapping strategy cannot be evaluated independently of link-level resource generation and quantum-memory dynamics. Protocol performance depends on how these processes interact across different operating regimes.

This study uses simulated four-hop repeater chains, Werner-state noise modeling, and six-state QKD secret-key-rate evaluation. Its conclusions apply to the modeled architectures and operating assumptions, rather than all possible quantum networks.
